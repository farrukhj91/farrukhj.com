The problem

The knowledge is there. Getting to it is the hard part.

Most of what a team knows was never written down. It sits with the few people who have done the job longest, and it moves only when they are free to move it.

Knowledge leaves when people do

People retire, change teams or take another job. Years of hard-won knowledge goes with them, and nobody can describe the gap until the day somebody needs it.

There is never time to share it

The person who knows is the person everyone needs. Explaining it properly always loses to the work only they can do.

One person cannot repeat it forever

Nobody can run the same walkthrough for every new joiner, and then again for the next batch. It ends up inconsistent, or skipped.

Complete learning support

Said once, written down, usable by everyone after.

Two halves of the same job: the courses people learn from, and the procedures they work to once they have.

Why it matters

Why learning systems matter

The value is not the document. It is what stops happening once the document exists.

Onboarding runs itself

A new joiner works through the same material as the last one, on their first day, without booking anybody’s time.

Everyone works one way

One written procedure replaces the local variants nobody knew were different until something broke.

The value compounds

Every course written this year still works next year, for people who have not been hired.

It survives the org chart

Teams reorganise and people move. The knowledge stays put rather than following whoever happened to hold it.

How We Build Learning Programs

We work to established instructional design frameworks rather than a house style, so material stays consistent, measurable and portable between teams and platforms.

ADDIE

Analyze, Design, Develop, Implement, Evaluate. Scope and success measures are agreed before production starts, and reviewed once the material is in use.

Bloom’s Taxonomy

Learning objectives are written against Bloom’s levels, so every module states what a learner should be able to do, not just what it covers.

SCORM-compliant delivery

Courses are packaged to the SCORM standard and tested in your LMS, so completions, scores and progress report correctly from day one.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is tribal knowledge, and why is it a risk?

It is everything your team knows how to do that nobody has written down. It works, right up until the person holding it retires, changes team or takes a week off, and then the work either stops or gets done a different way. The risk is not that people leave. It is that nothing was captured before they did.

Where do we start if almost nothing is documented?

With whatever hurts most, usually onboarding or the process that goes wrong most often. We sit with the people who actually do the work rather than waiting for them to write it up themselves, because that is the step that never happens.

What is the difference between instructional design and SOP documentation?

Instructional design is how somebody learns to do the work: structured courses, delivered in a room or over video, with the material that goes around them. SOP documentation is what they follow once they have learned it. Most teams need both, and the two are separate pages on this site.

Do you follow SCORM and instructional design frameworks?

Yes. We build SCORM-compliant material and work to established frameworks including ADDIE and Bloom's Taxonomy, as set out further up this page.

Can you work with our existing training material?

Yes. Existing material is usually the starting point rather than something to throw away.

Which platforms do you build learning content in?

The platforms listed at the foot of this page, alongside whatever your team already uses.
