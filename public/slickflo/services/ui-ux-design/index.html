Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between UI and UX design?

UX is the structure: the flows, the journey, and whether someone can get where they are going. UI is the surface: the screens, the type, the spacing and how it all behaves. A product can have a handsome interface and still lose people, which is usually a UX problem wearing a UI coat. We work on both because they fail together.

We already have a developer. Why do we need UI/UX design?

Because a developer builds to the specification and a designer builds to the use, and those are not the same thing. That gap is what this page is about: the product gets built exactly as described and users still cannot find their way through it.

Do you design for SaaS products and startups specifically?

Yes, that is most of what we do. SaaS products tend to fail on the same things, an onboarding nobody can finish and a site that describes features without giving anyone a reason to buy.

What does user research actually involve?

Research into the people who will use the product rather than the people who imagined it, messaging tested against the customer it is aimed at, and an in depth analysis of competing products so you are not re-teaching a lesson the market has already learned. It is the first thing cut and the only thing that tells you whether an obvious idea is a good one.

How long does UI/UX design take?

Less than it used to. We design in Figma and go straight to a working prototype, so you see the real thing in days rather than weeks. The point of that speed is not the invoice, it is that research and iteration stop being the first things cut for time.

What is vibe design?

Using AI to get from an idea to something you can click on, without the weeks that used to sit in between. In practice it means AI assisted research, rapid prototyping, and working code generated straight from the designs. The tools we use for it are in our tech arsenal on this page.

Can you redesign an existing product rather than starting over?

Usually, and it is often the cheaper answer. The work starts the same way either way, by finding where interest turns into confusion, which on a live product you can see rather than guess at.

Do you build the website as well, or only design it?

Both, though they are separate pieces of work. This page covers the interface, the flows and the messaging. Building and running the site, templates, CMS and performance, is on our website design and development page.
