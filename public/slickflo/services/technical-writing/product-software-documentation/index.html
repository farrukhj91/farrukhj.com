Documentation That Aligns Product & Technical Teams

Clear product documentation plays a critical role in aligning stakeholders, reducing ambiguity, and ensuring smoother product execution across engineering, product, QA, and operational teams.

SlickFlo’s product documentation services are tailored to system workflows, technical requirements, and implementation processes. They are one part of our technical writing services, focused on the documents your team builds from.

From product requirements documents (PRDs), software requirements specifications (SRS), functional specifications and technical design documents to use cases, workflows, system documentation and release notes, our technical documentation services focus on clarity, consistency and cross-functional alignment throughout the product lifecycle. For SaaS teams that ship every few weeks, that means SaaS documentation that keeps pace with the release cycle.

Release Notes

Release notes are written from the build, not from the ticket.

A ticket says what was meant to change. Release notes have to say what actually did. We work the new features the way a customer will, confirm what shipped against what was specified, and write the note from that. It is why release notes sit with QA rather than beside them, and why the note matches the build people download.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does product and software documentation cover?

The documents product and engineering teams build from, and the notes customers read when something ships: product requirements documents (PRDs), software requirements specifications (SRS), functional specifications, technical design documents, use cases, workflow and system documentation, and release notes. Customer-facing user guides, API documentation and knowledge bases each have their own page.

Who is it for?

Product managers, engineering leads, QA teams and founders who need everyone working from the same definition of the product. Most of these documents are internal. Release notes are the exception: they are written for customers and the teams who support them.

Do you work inside our development process?

Yes. We work in the tools your team already uses, such as Jira, Confluence and Figma, so the documentation stays part of the process rather than sitting outside it.

What is the difference between a PRD and an SRS?

A PRD describes what the product should do and why: the problem, the users, the goals and the features in scope. An SRS describes how the software must behave to meet those goals, with functional and non-functional requirements written precisely enough for engineering to build from and QA to test against. Many teams use both, with the PRD coming first.

Can you write specifications for a product that is already built?

Yes. Many teams ship before the specifications are written down. Our product documentation services cover the product as it actually works, from the product itself and short sessions with your team, so new engineers, QA and partners have an accurate reference to build on.
