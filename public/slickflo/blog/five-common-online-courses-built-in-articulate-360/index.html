Five Common Online Courses Built in Articulate 360

By Qutab Baig, Co-Founder at SlickFlo18 November 2026

What would you teach your team if you could create any course, you wanted and put it in front of them? Many organizations today have moved to that point. Tools like Articulate 360 put course creation within reach, and organizations are capitalizing on them to train their employees and audiences. SHRM’s 2026 study found that external training’s share of L&D budgets dropped from 25% to 15%, as more development came in-house.

Perhaps you have felt that your organization needs its own custom courses to be developed, one centered on your products, processes or values. If you are now asking which courses to build, read on. This blog is for you.

At SlickFlo, we build e-learning courses in Articulate 360 for businesses with different models, sizes and goals. Below are five types of courses we proudly published, with why each is needed, what we build and what it delivers.

Staff onboarding and training courses

A dental group with clinics across the US, all offering a wide range of dental services, came to SlickFlo with a misalignment problem.

The challenge: The chief dental officer knew which treatments to recommend, but the first patient conversation happens at the front desk, with a treatment coordinator or receptionist. Leadership cannot join every one of those conversations, so that knowledge had to reach all patient-facing staff. New doctors also needed to learn the group's preferred products and processes.

What SlickFlo did: We created the group's courses from scratch in Articulate Rise 360 and Storyline 360, covering onboarding, staff training and on-the-job learning. We also defined the processes behind them: how new courses are created, what they should look like, and the SOPs that support them. Finally, we set controls for learners to sustain consistent course completion.

The result: Every clinic now works from the same playbook. Instead of one location offering one thing and another something else, all staff know what to offer, what to suggest and which standards to follow. That consistency grew as more employees completed their training, with average completion rising from 40% to over 90%. New hires also reach confident patient conversations faster.

Courses companies sell

A UK-based organization that builds and sells leadership courses wanted to open a new revenue stream with an online course of its own. It had the vision, but it wanted an expert to build the course for it, so it came to us.

The challenge: The course was a first of its kind: leadership for teenagers. The organization had the idea, the scripts and the funding. What it did not have was someone to build it. The team knew what the course should say, but not how to build it well, make it scalable or put the right controls in place. The bigger concern was engagement: a course for teenagers must not feel like a school assignment.

What SlickFlo did: We designed the course around its audience. The illustrations, the layout and the interactive elements suit teenagers, and the examples come from situations they recognize. We also built it to scale, so one course can serve many learners.

The result: Teenagers responded strongly to the course and stayed involved from start to finish, which matters commercially: a course learners enjoy is one they complete and talk about. For the organization, the build was a one-time investment that it can sell again and again, so every new learner adds revenue at very little extra cost.

Franchise training courses

An Australian organization with an established franchise network wanted its franchisees to do more than deliver the product well. It came to us to build a course that every franchisee would enroll in.

The challenge: Franchisees were doing a good job with the product. The gaps were on the business side: sales, communication, soft skills and the decisions that drive revenue. Operations training rarely covers these, and one poor decision at one location can tarnish the reputation of the whole brand, not only that franchisee.

What SlickFlo did: We developed a course that franchisees enroll in themselves. It covers the brand's standards and daily operations, and also what is rarely taught: how a unit makes money, how to generate revenue and how to communicate with customers. Every franchisee gets the same playbook.

The result: Decisions no longer depend on what the manager on duty decides from personal experience. They follow the organization's own values, which protects the brand's reputation. Quality, service and delivery stay consistent at every location.

Sales and product enablement courses

A global organization with a long history of acquiring companies came to us with a growing catalog: products it built itself and products that arrived with its acquisitions.

The challenge: The sales cycle could not pause while people learned. New hires had to learn existing products, and existing teams had to learn new ones, with no time for cohorts. Salespeople needed to work independently. Documentation covered the features, but not the messaging: how to sell each product and which audience to target.

What SlickFlo did: We built a series of courses for each product, from an introduction to its features. Sales engineers learn the product quickly, then move on to selling or supporting, depending on their role.

The result: Sales and support teams were onboarded quickly, with consistent messaging across every product, built or acquired. The sales cycle kept moving.

After the course, questions keep coming, and a knowledge base answers them. See our blog posts on using an online knowledgebase to increase product adoption and 7 things to know before writing knowledgebase articles.

Annual compliance training courses

Every organization has training that is not optional: security, harassment, privacy and safety, plus its own internal policies.

The challenge: Many organizations must put every employee through this training each year, some because the law requires it and some because internal policy does. The hard part is not assigning it. It is making sure everyone completes it, understands it and can prove it, whatever team or location they work in.

What SlickFlo does: We create compliance courses that people have a reason to finish. Interactive scenarios give learners something to do, and reporting records completion in your LMS. Our instructional design team aligns the content with your own policies.

The result: Employees complete the training that the law and your organizational policies require. You keep a clear record of who has completed it and who still needs to, including new hires and their deadlines.

Conclusion

Creating a course is easier today than it has ever been, but creating one that works is still a skill. Whether your learners are employees, students or an audience building a new skill, they invest time in every course you publish. You invest time creating it. The course has to repay both, with outcomes you can see.

At SlickFlo, we help organizations achieve their learning and development goal. We start by finding the gaps, both in the training and in the goals the course must reach. Our instructional design services follow five steps: Discover, Structure, Develop, Configure and Optimize. We package the course to SCORM and set it up end-to-end in your LMS.
